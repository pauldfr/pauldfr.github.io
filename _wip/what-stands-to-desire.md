title: "What Stands to Desire as Perception Stands to Belief?"

order: 1

abstract: "Perceptual systems regulate our beliefs in response to experience by generating signals that track features of the environment. I ask what, if anything, plays an analogous role for desire. Drawing on computational cognitive science, I argue that we possess a quasi-perceptual capacity that I call <i>conaception</i>. This capacity functions as a feature-detector, generating non-conceptual value signals that update subpersonal value representations governing our desires, much as perceptual signals update belief. I further argue that conaception is a reliable process: it tracks what is good for us—what is conducive to our well-being—in much the same way perceptual capacities reliably track properties such as distance. Because conaception is typically accurate, desires shaped by it enjoy a defeasible normative authority, just as beliefs generated by our perceptual capacities do. By contrast, desires produced by motivational illusions—cases in which conaception misfires—or by other unreliable processes, such as some mental illnesses, lack this normative authority. This explains why we are usually, but not always, rational in relying on our ordinary desires, and how engaging in practical deliberation can allow us to improve them."
