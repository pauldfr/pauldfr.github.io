title: "A Conflation of Valences"

abstract: "There is a common assumption that humans and other agents learn what to do from the valence of their experiences. This view has seemingly been vindicated by recent developments in computational cognitive science, apparently implying motivational hedonism. In this paper, I argue that this view is based on a conflation between two distinct notions of valence: learning valence, which determines future attraction or aversion, and hedonic valence, the felt quality of pleasure or displeasure. I present a range of evidence that these refer to different mental kinds. This means that, contrary to the suggestion above, motivational hedonism is false. It also means that the capacity to learn from trial and error is no evidence of sentience, contrary to some arguments for attributing sentience to simple animals."
