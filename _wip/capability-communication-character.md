abstract: "Evaluating the epistemic performance of foundation models is a critical challenge for the field of AI safety. While existing benchmarks capture various dimensions of performance, there is currently no unified normative framework for what constitutes a good epistemic agent. In this paper, I argue that mere accuracy is insufficient for epistemic virtue. I propose a systematic framework grounded in formal epistemology and statistical theory, breaking down epistemic performance into three pillars: Capability, Communication, and Character. I show that a robust epistemic agent must not only possess accurate internal representations (Capability) but must also empower its users (Communication) and demonstrate reliability across contexts (Character). I conclude that this framework provides a necessary roadmap for developing benchmarks with high construct validity."
